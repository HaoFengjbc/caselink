BACKGROUND AND PROCEDURAL HISTORY
The supplied April 2022 report concerns journalist Aigul Utepova's prosecution under Article 405(2) for
alleged participation in the activities of banned opposition groups, the Democratic Choice of Kazakhstan
and Koshe Party. The prosecution relied on political commentary and social-media activity; the report
found no allegation that she advocated violence. She disputed that reporting or commenting on the groups
amounted to participation in them. 1
Following arrest in September 2020, she spent months under house arrest and underwent involuntary
psychiatric confinement from 23 November to 11 December 2020. Release from the clinic was a distinct
event and did not establish release from house arrest. The report examined the justification and
safeguards for confinement as well as the later trial. 1
On 29 April 2021, the trial court imposed one year of restricted movement, a two-year ban on social-media
use and public activism, and 100 hours of compulsory labor. Her conviction appeal was rejected in June
2021. The report also documented inclusion on a terrorism-financing-related list and blocked bank access.
These are historical findings, not verification that the same restraints remain active in 2026. 1
TrialWatch expert Dr. Eva Szeli assigned the original grade D. The report identified pretrial and trial rights
violations, problems with expert evidence and disclosure, and the vague, overbroad application of
anti-extremism rules to nonviolent expression. The present follow-up attributes those conclusions to the
original report without giving the case a new grade. 1

SUBSEQUENT DEVELOPMENTS
CFJ's April 2022 statement says the communication sought reversal of the conviction, an apology and
reparations, as well as reform of anti-extremism laws. Those are the representatives' requested remedies,
not relief already granted by the Committee. The same statement describes frozen bank access and
health and professional harm as they were understood at that time. 2
The 2025 NGO submission confirms registration and then-pending status, not a Committee decision. Its
session date, rather than later search indexing, dates the account. 3
CPJ's historical profile records rejection of the appeal on 1 June 2021 and says Utepova had not yet
submitted her planned UN complaint as of October 2021. That earlier statement is consistent with a
subsequent filing announced in April 2022, rather than evidence against it. The profile should not be read
as a live prisoner-status record. 5
