EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
LATEST VERIFIED POSITION
Pardoned in 2019; no subsequent acquittal or vacatur verified
The latest located subject-specific follow-up is a published interview, not a new judicial disposition. The pardon
and release occurred on 16 October 2019 and were already recorded in the original report. The record located
through 9 October 2026 does not establish that the convictions were subsequently quashed.
Hajar Raissouni and her co-defendants received a royal pardon on 16 October 2019. This was not a
post-report development: the August 2020 TrialWatch report already distinguished the pardon from an
acquittal. Amnesty International likewise reported that the convictions had not been quashed. The
appropriate follow-up status is therefore pardon and release, without a verified later judicial clearing of the
case. 1 2
The latest located direct follow-up is Raissouni's interview published on 2 November 2022, describing
post-release harassment and her subsequent life in Paris. Her earlier first-person account also described
leaving Morocco. These accounts establish reported continuing consequences, not a new prosecution, a
judicial finding of surveillance, or a 2026 determination of her circumstances. 4 5

BACKGROUND AND PROCEDURAL HISTORY
The original report concerned five defendants arrested in connection with Raissouni's medical treatment
on 31 August 2019: Raissouni, her fiance Rifaat Al Amine, doctor Jamal Belkeziz, anesthesiologist
Mohamed Baba, and secretary Khadija Azelmadh. Raissouni was prosecuted for abortion and sexual
relations outside marriage under Articles 454 and 490 of the Penal Code. On 30 September 2019, she
and her fiance received one-year prison sentences; the doctor received two years; the other two
defendants received suspended eight-month sentences. All five were pardoned on 16 October. 1
TrialWatch expert Baroness Helena Kennedy assigned the proceedings a D grade. The report identified
an invasive medical examination without consent, impediments to counsel, judicial impartiality concerns,
contested police statements treated as confessions, and restrictions on defense evidence. Its assessment
that the prosecution implicated discriminatory enforcement and Raissouni's journalism is the original
expert's assessment, not a newly established judicial finding. The report explicitly stated that pardon did
not erase conviction. 1

SUBSEQUENT DEVELOPMENTS
The 2021 MENA Rights Group update documents an actual September 2019 submission and a
subsequent Special Procedures communication, rather than merely an intention to seek UN assistance.
Both actions preceded the original report. The profile's later update confirms the release classification but
does not supply a subsequent court judgment or a UN merits decision. 3
In her August 2021 essay, Raissouni described harassment after release, an initial obstacle to leaving
Morocco that was later resolved, and continued attacks while abroad. She also discussed her and her
husband's numbers appearing in leaked Pegasus-related records. That account should not be converted
into a claim of independently verified infection of either device. 4
The November 2022 interview provides a later personal account of surveillance-like pressure following the
pardon and relocation abroad. The source's introductory wording associates release with 17 October,
whereas the original report and Amnesty identify 16 October as the operative pardon/release date. This
follow-up retains the better-corroborated date and treats the interview as evidence of her reported
experience, not adjudicated responsibility. 1 2 5

CONCLUSION AND OUTSTANDING RECORDS
The report should retain the original D grade as historical attribution and identify the 2019 pardon as the
established disposition. Post-report developments concern Raissouni's reported harassment and
relocation, rather than a verified reversal. The absence of a located newer judgment is a research
limitation, not proof that no further legal action occurred. 1 4 5
RECORDS NOT VERIFIED
1. Was any conviction later vacated, expunged, or otherwise removed from the criminal record? Obtain
the operative pardon instrument and any subsequent court or rehabilitation order.
2. Did any defendant pursue an appeal or compensation claim after the pardon, and what was its
disposition?
3. What happened to the professional sanctions, fines, and records of the doctor and clinic staff? The later
subject interviews do not answer this.
4. Was there any later response or substantive determination following UN communication AL MAR
4/2019? The located profile establishes intervention, not final resolution.
5. No subject-specific legal event later than the located follow-up accounts was verified through the cutoff.
Current residence and safety require fresher direct confirmation.
Failure to locate an update leaves a gap in the available evidence; it does not establish that no later proceeding or change
occurred. Any present-status claim should be read with the dated scope stated in the executive summary.
