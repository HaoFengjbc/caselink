BACKGROUND AND PROCEDURAL HISTORY
The supplied April 2022 report concerns journalist Alexander Pichugin, also transliterated Aleksandr, and
his Telegram channel Sorokin Khvost. It is not the case of imprisoned former Yukos employee Alexey
Pichugin. The subject and channel, the 300,000-ruble fine, and counsel's identity provide safeguards
against that consequential name confusion. 1
The prosecution arose from a satirical 12 April 2020 post criticizing church gatherings during the
COVID-19 pandemic. Under Article 207.1, authorities treated the commentary as knowingly false
information about a public threat. On 11 November 2020, the Bogorodsk City Court convicted Pichugin
and imposed a 300,000-ruble fine. It did not impose a custodial sentence. The TrialWatch monitoring
covered proceedings from July to November 2020. 1
TrialWatch expert Oliver Wallasch assigned the historical grade D. The report identified shortcomings in
proof of intent, treatment of linguistic evidence and reasoning, including the handling of evidence
supporting a metaphorical reading of the post. It also assessed the interference with freedom of
expression. Those are the original report's findings, not a fresh grading exercise or a claim that later
courts adopted every TrialWatch criticism. 1

SUBSEQUENT DEVELOPMENTS
Contemporaneous reporting confirms that the regional appeal was rejected on 26 January 2021. Counsel
Tumas Misakyan then intended to pursue cassation and Strasbourg proceedings. An announced intention
alone would not establish filing; the later ECtHR appendix supplies the application number and actual
introduction date. 2 3
The ECtHR found disproportionate interference with Article 10 in the satire prosecution and expressly
states that its committee judgment is final. 3
The representing lawyers describe separate protest-related findings under Articles 5, 6 and 11, alongside
the speech finding. The EUR 10,000 award covers the combined complaints rather than the original
prosecution alone. 4
The lawyer organization's same-day account independently ties the European decision to the journalist
and original post. It explains the combined rights findings and compensation award, reinforcing
identification while not proving that the award was paid or that Russian courts removed the conviction. 4
Kommersant's November 2025 report connects the foreign-agent designation to the same journalist and
the COVID satire conviction. It also reports that he had lived outside Russia since 2022. That is a dated
account of relocation, not verification of his residence or legal security on 9 October 2026. The
government's allegations associated with designation should not be treated as established facts. 5
