BACKGROUND AND PROCEDURAL HISTORY
The January 2026 original concerned Amaglobeli, founder of Batumelebi and Netgazeti, following her
arrest during protests in Batumi in January 2025. The prosecution initially charged an attack on a police
officer. On 6 August 2025 the court convicted her instead under Article 353(1), concerning resistance to
an officer, and sentenced her to two years. The Kutaisi Court of Appeal upheld that result on 18 November
2025. The original already covered those appellate hearings; they are not post-report discoveries. 1
TrialWatch expert Sir Nicolas Bratza assigned a D. The assessment identified inadequate notice of the
substituted charge, impaired access to defense materials and witnesses, participation problems
associated with the courtroom glass enclosure, prejudicial statements by public officials, and inadequate
justification for imprisonment. This follow-up attributes those findings to the original report rather than
issuing a new grade or independently adopting every allegation as judicially established. 1

SUBSEQUENT DEVELOPMENTS
CPJ reported nonadmission on 4 March, citing her lawyers' criticism that the Supreme Court had not
addressed their admissibility arguments. CPJ also recorded an intention to appeal to Strasbourg. That
statement alone would not prove a filing; the later records must supply the missing procedural step. 2
GYLA's 14 January update identified two different Strasbourg proceedings already in existence:
application 13661/25, filed on 28 April 2025 concerning criminal detention, and application 36304/25, filed
on 17 November 2025 concerning administrative detention. In the first, the government had submitted
observations in October 2025 and GYLA had responded by January. The alleged violations were the
applicants' claims, not Strasbourg findings. 3
Batumelebi reported a third application alleging police ill-treatment and deficiencies in its investigation. It
was submitted on 4 March 2026 and registered on 11 March, with notification received on 1 April. Sharing
a date with the Supreme Court decision does not make this complaint the conviction appeal. 4
The Messenger subsequently reported GYLA's announcement of a fourth complaint: the conviction and
fair-trial challenge, submitted on 26 June and registered on 3 July. It concerned the prosecution and
proceedings at all three domestic court levels. The account identified GYLA and Edward Fitzgerald as
representatives. The filing is verified through reporting of counsel's statement, not an independently
retrieved Strasbourg docket. 5
In a prison letter published on 11 September, Amaglobeli said she was awaiting international justice and
rejected seeking a pardon through an apology. CMIS's later publication also reported refusal of early
release and obstacles to visiting her. Neither source identifies a subsequent favorable judgment; the
precise early-release decision date and written reasons remain unverified. 6 7

CONCLUSION AND OUTSTANDING RECORDS
The verified follow-up is unsuccessful access to domestic cassation and multiple actual Strasbourg
applications, including the conviction challenge. The public record located does not justify describing
Amaglobeli as released, pardoned or exonerated.
RECORDS NOT VERIFIED
1. Supreme Court's complete nonadmission order and precise admissibility reasoning.
2. Application number and official procedural status of the June 2026 conviction complaint and March
2026 ill-treatment complaint.
3. Any ECHR admissibility, interim-measure or merits ruling; registration is not such a ruling.
4. Date, decision-maker, written reasons and review status of the reported early-release refusal.
5. Custody confirmation covering the interval after the September publication through 9 October, or any
intervening release order.
Failure to locate an update leaves a gap in the available evidence; it does not establish that no later proceeding or change
occurred. Any present-status claim should be read with the dated scope stated in the executive summary.
