BACKGROUND AND PROCEDURAL HISTORY
The prosecution arose from Piskin's 5 April 2018 report about the investigation of Bogazici University
students, identifying prosecutor Ercan Guclu. Istanbul's 36th High Criminal Court applied Article 6(1) of
Anti-Terror Law No. 3713, concerning identification or targeting of officials engaged in counterterrorism.
The defense argued that the name was already public and that the required intent had not been
established. The original also questioned reopening a previously declined prosecution without new
evidence. 1
Ambassador Stephen J. Rapp awarded the proceedings a D grade. The original criticized the failure to
address material defense arguments and the interference with freedom of expression. It recorded deferred
pronouncement, commonly called HAGB, with a five-year supervisory period, rather than immediate
service of ten months in prison. It also explained the restrictions on ordinary appeal associated with
accepting deferment under the law applicable to this proceeding. That historical description should not be
silently replaced by assumptions about later procedural reforms. 1

SUBSEQUENT DEVELOPMENTS
The contemporaneous accounts confirm deferment rather than an acquittal. MLSA's headline and
substantive sentencing description, bianet, CPJ and the original agree on ten months. MLSA's
introductory text contains an inconsistent reference to ten years; it is not adopted here. 1 2 3 4
The later sources document professional activity, not litigation progress in Piskin's case. Searches in
English and Turkish for objections, constitutional proceedings, acquittal and dismissal did not yield an
attributable later decision. That negative search result leaves the disposition unverified; it cannot establish
that no decision exists. 5 6

CONCLUSION AND OUTSTANDING RECORDS
The defensible update is limited: the 2019 deferred judgment is established, but its later disposition is not.
Retain the original D grade with attribution. Do not label Piskin acquitted, currently imprisoned, or
definitively cleared without a case-specific order. 1
RECORDS NOT VERIFIED
1. Was an objection to the HAGB order filed, and was it accepted or rejected? Obtain the objection record
rather than assuming an ordinary appeal.
2. When did the supervisory period legally begin, and was any order issued dismissing the prosecution or
activating the sentence?
3. Did Piskin file an individual constitutional application or an ECHR application concerning this
prosecution, and did either produce a decision? No actual filing or result was verified.
4. Can counsel or the court provide the later docket entries and final criminal-record consequences?
Public reporting alone did not resolve these questions.
Failure to locate an update leaves a gap in the available evidence; it does not establish that no later proceeding or change
occurred. Any present-status claim should be read with the dated scope stated in the executive summary.
