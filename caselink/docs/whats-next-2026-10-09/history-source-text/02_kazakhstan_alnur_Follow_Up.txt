BACKGROUND AND PROCEDURAL HISTORY
The supplied March 2021 report concerns Facebook criticism of the ruling Nur Otan party and the
government's COVID-19 response. Ilyashev was arrested on 17 April 2020 and prosecuted under Article
274 for allegedly spreading false information during a state of emergency. The monitored trial took place
in June 2020 by videoconference, with repeated connection failures and concerns about confidential
access to counsel and the effective examination of witnesses and expert evidence. 1
On 22 June 2020 the court imposed three years of restricted liberty, compulsory labor, and a five-year
prohibition on civic or public activities. Restricted liberty was not an acquittal or an unconditional release.
The Almaty City Court upheld the conviction on 15 September 2020; the original report also records
unsuccessful Supreme Court review. The content and reach of the additional activity ban were central
concerns because it potentially restricted protected expression and association beyond the
movement-related sentence. 1
TrialWatch expert Vania Costa Ramos assigned the original grade D. Her assessment addressed fairness
defects and the disproportionate prosecution of political criticism. The historical grade is attributed to that
report only. No later development is used here to calculate a replacement TrialWatch score. 1

SUBSEQUENT DEVELOPMENTS
The Working Group classified the deprivation of liberty as arbitrary under categories I, II, III and V. It
requested quashing the sentence, expunging the criminal record, compensation and other reparations,
and an independent investigation. This is a verified international finding, not evidence that Kazakhstan
implemented those measures. 2
Freedom Now's case page expressly dates release, avoiding an inference from the nominal sentence
length. In his April 2025 Vlast interview, Ilyashev described renewed work as a consultant but continuing
inability to join the legal-consultants chamber because of his criminal record. He said the public/political
ban would expire that year; the interview does not itself certify its eventual termination. 3 4
TrialWatch's 2025 submission identifies a separate Human Rights Committee communication as
submitted but awaiting registration. No later Committee disposition was verified. 7
The 2026 investigation was reported as fraud-related, not an Article 274 speech prosecution. The most
reliable accessible accounts identify witness status rather than a proven offence. The October
monitoring's publication date should not be mistaken for a new October detention. 6

CONCLUSION AND OUTSTANDING RECORDS
The documented trajectory is international condemnation, reported release, and continuing collateral
concerns, followed by a distinct 2026 investigation. As of the cutoff, a domestic reversal, expungement,
implemented reparations, and the outcome of the new investigation remain unverified. 2 4 5

2. Were compensation or other reparations implemented?

removed?
4. What is the registration number and subsequent status of the separate Human Rights Committee
communication?
5. Did the September 2026 fraud investigation alter Ilyashev's witness status or impose any later
restrictions?
