BACKGROUND AND PROCEDURAL HISTORY
The supplied January 2021 report concerns a Pskov journalist's commentary on the October 2018 suicide
bombing at the FSB building in Arkhangelsk. Her discussion connected the attack to political repression
and the absence of ordinary outlets for protest. The prosecution treated that contextual analysis as public
justification of terrorism under Article 205.2(2), rather than identifying an express appeal by her to commit
violence. 1
On 6 July 2020, the Second Western District Military Court convicted Prokopyeva and imposed a
500,000-ruble fine and confiscation of her laptop. The prosecutor had sought six years' imprisonment and
a four-year journalism ban, but those requested penalties were not the sentence imposed. The report
therefore must not be summarized as a six-year custodial conviction. 1
Covington & Burling LLP assigned the original TrialWatch grade D. The report identified problems with
impartiality, expert evidence, treatment of defence arguments and proof of the elements of the offence,
alongside serious freedom-of-expression concerns. The baseline also described financial consequences
associated with the terrorism/extremism list. Later delisting and subsequent foreign-agent proceedings
must be assessed separately rather than treating all restrictive registers as one status. 1

SUBSEQUENT DEVELOPMENTS
Interfax expressly reports Rosfinmonitoring's removal of her name on 19 May 2022. Delisting is not the
same as quashing the conviction, but it makes a blanket claim that the original terrorism-list restriction
simply continued misleading. No verified later re-entry was located. 2
RFE/RL reported in July 2024 that the Interior Ministry's wanted database listed Prokopyeva on
unspecified charges. It also described her March 2022 move to Latvia after a search and questioning in
another case. Because the database did not specify charges, that report cannot establish a new terrorism
prosecution or an arrest warrant's precise legal basis. 3
The ECtHR appendix identifies application 60839/21 and the original bombing-commentary prosecution.
Findings concerning other joined applicants should not be transferred to her case. 4
April reporting described a separate 30,000-ruble foreign-agent-label fine. The defence denied liability. 5
The May report confirms receipt of the defence appeal but dates the original order to 21 April, after the
initial report's 20 April publication. The ruling is needed to resolve this discrepancy. 6
