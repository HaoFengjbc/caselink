BACKGROUND AND PROCEDURAL HISTORY
The February 2024 report concerned the founder of elPeriodico, arrested on 29 July 2022 and tried for
money laundering, influence peddling and blackmail. In June 2023 he received a six-year
money-laundering sentence and was acquitted of the other two charges. In October 2023 an appeals
court annulled both the conviction and acquittals and ordered a retrial. At publication, cassation
proceedings remained pending; the report did not establish a final conviction. 1
TrialWatch expert Camilo Sanchez assigned an F. The report identified serious restrictions on defense
evidence, treatment of the burden of proof, interference with legal representation and deficiencies in the
review and conditions of detention. Several defense lawyers themselves faced proceedings. The
shutdown of elPeriodico in May 2023 formed part of the documented consequences. The later history
must therefore track custody, evidentiary remedies and the merits separately rather than treating every
release decision as resolution of the criminal case. 1

SUBSEQUENT DEVELOPMENTS
The route out of prison was reversible. AP's March 2025 account recorded actual house arrest in October
2024, an adverse appellate ruling in November, and return to prison on 10 March 2025. On 12 February
2026 CPJ confirmed another move to house arrest while the criminal proceedings continued. None of
these custody decisions alone disposed of the allegations. 3 2
On 28 July, Prensa Libre reported acceptance of the prosecution's total withdrawal of its constitutional
appeal. The challenged Supreme Court amparo, granted on 20 March, had restored substitute measures.
The withdrawn appeal could not be renewed. This particular proceeding ended, not the prosecution or all
possible custody litigation. 4
In a separate report the same day, Prensa Comunitaria described a renewed appellate annulment of the
2023 judgment and referral for retrial before Tribunal Noveno. It followed a reported October 2025
Criminal Chamber instruction to reconsider the appellate reasoning. The article also discussed the
treatment of excluded defense evidence and co-defendant Samari Gomez. The full operative decision is
needed to establish the exact scope of the retrial and any unresolved challenges. 5
On 13 August, Prensa Libre reported a majority refusal to lift Zamora's exit restriction for medical
treatment in Florida, although prosecutors did not oppose the request. The court considered the proposed
treatment insufficiently urgent. The same report said the retrial had not started and that Gomez had
challenged the retrial order on 11 August; separating their proceedings was refused. These are attributed
accounts of ongoing litigation, not proof that a new trial had concluded. 6
The National Press Club's 8 October statement supplies a more recent custody check than the February
release announcement. It named Zamora as a press-freedom award recipient while explicitly describing
continuing house arrest and the defense of outstanding charges. The proposed November award
ceremony was still future at the cutoff and is not treated as an event already held. 7

CONCLUSION AND OUTSTANDING RECORDS
As of the cutoff, the best-supported characterization is house arrest with ongoing criminal proceedings
and a reported retrial order. There is no verified retrial verdict or unconditional release. The most recent
confirmation is dated 8 October, not the earlier February custody decision.
RECORDS NOT VERIFIED
1. Certified July appellate and constitutional decisions, including their exact issuance dates and operative
scope.
2. Disposition of Gomez's reported 11 August challenge and its effect on Zamora's retrial timetable.
3. Whether the retrial began after 13 August, which charges remain for retrial, and which defense
evidence is admissible.
4. Current conditions of house arrest, exit restriction and any other independent pending prosecutions.
5. Any later medical-travel authorization, unconditional release, dismissal or retrial judgment before the
cutoff; none was verified.
Failure to locate an update leaves a gap in the available evidence; it does not establish that no later proceeding or change
occurred. Any present-status claim should be read with the dated scope stated in the executive summary.
