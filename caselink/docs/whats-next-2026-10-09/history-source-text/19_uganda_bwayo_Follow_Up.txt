BACKGROUND AND PROCEDURAL HISTORY
Bwayo was filming opposition politician and musician Robert Kyagulanyi, known as Bobi Wine, on a
Kampala hotel rooftop on 24 February 2020 when police detained him. He was released on police bond
on 26 February, arrested again and charged on 4 March with eight other people, remanded to Luzira
Prison, and granted bail on 6 March. The charge was unlawful assembly. On 10 August, the Makindye
Chief Magistrate's Court declined another adjournment and dismissed the prosecution after witnesses
failed to appear. 1
The ABA Center for Human Rights staff experts assigned the proceedings a C grade. The report identified
concerns about the arrests, detention, charging basis and prosecution's failure to advance the case.
Dismissal was not a merits judgment establishing innocence. The original also discussed Bwayo being
shot in the face while filming in November 2020; because that event preceded the report, it must not be
presented as newly discovered post-report violence. 1

SUBSEQUENT DEVELOPMENTS
ABA's July 2021 webpage reiterates the historical dismissal. It is a later publication of the existing result,
not evidence of a second proceeding. The November 2023 Irish Times profile independently recalls that
the case was eventually dropped, while reporting that Bwayo had left Uganda and was seeking asylum in
the United States. It also describes interference with his filmmaking. 2 3
Bwayo's April 2024 essay supplies the more specific March 2022 relocation date. He describes threats,
two attempted kidnappings of his wife, and the risks of return. These are attributed firsthand allegations,
not adjudicated findings. His reference to seeking asylum supports an application-stage description only;
the research did not establish a grant. The official Sundance selection announcement later supplies
independent evidence of continued film work. 4 5
A Ugandan High Court cause list generated on 7 November 2025 names a Moses Bwayo against the
Inspector General of Police and Attorney General in civil matter HCT-00-CV-MC-0019-2022, seeking
declaratory orders and costs. This is an unresolved identity and linkage lead. No located pleading or
judgment establishes that the claimant is this filmmaker or that the claim concerns the 2020 arrest. A
cause list does not prove that the listed hearing occurred or disclose an outcome. It is therefore excluded
from the verified case chronology. 6

CONCLUSION AND OUTSTANDING RECORDS
The supported legal status remains the original August 2020 dismissal, with later reported exile and
continued filmmaking. Retain the original C grade with attribution. Further conclusions about
reinstatement, asylum status or civil redress require records not obtained in this research. 1 4 5
RECORDS NOT VERIFIED
1. Was the unlawful-assembly charge ever reinstated, or was the dismissal challenged? Obtain the later
Makindye docket entries.
2. What is the current asylum status? Seeking asylum in the 2023-2024 accounts is not evidence that it
was granted.
3. Is the claimant in HCT-00-CV-MC-0019-2022 the filmmaker, and does the matter concern the arrests
monitored by TrialWatch? Obtain the originating motion and any ruling before attributing it.
4. Was there an investigation, accountability decision or compensation concerning the arrests or
November 2020 shooting? No attributable outcome was verified.
5. Did any of the eight co-accused have a different subsequent outcome? The subject-focused later
sources do not establish their status.
Failure to locate an update leaves a gap in the available evidence; it does not establish that no later proceeding or change
occurred. Any present-status claim should be read with the dated scope stated in the executive summary.
