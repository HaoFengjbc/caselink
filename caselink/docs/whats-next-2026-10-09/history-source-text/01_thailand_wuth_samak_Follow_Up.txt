BACKGROUND AND PROCEDURAL HISTORY
The supplied July 2020 report concerns a criminal-defamation prosecution arising from Facebook
commentary about alleged land-related misconduct by an official. Samak was accused of publishing the
posts, while Wuth was accused of sharing them without adding commentary. The allegedly defamatory
passages did not expressly name Chaiwat. The charge was brought under section 328 of Thailand's
Criminal Code, and the proceedings also exposed the defendants to a private compensation claim. Both
defendants were human-rights defenders whose work addressed Karen communities and land rights. 1
TrialWatch monitored hearings between September and November 2019. On 18 November 2019, the trial
court acquitted both defendants of all charges and rejected the co-plaintiff's compensation demand. The
report nevertheless distinguished an acquittal from a prosecution free of rights concerns: the defendants
had already borne the burdens of criminal proceedings over public-interest speech. 1
Lionel Blackman, the report's TrialWatch expert, assigned the original grade C. The assessment
described generally fair trial procedures but questioned the prosecution and acceptance of a weak
criminal-defamation case against activists. Its concern was not that the defendants had received prison
sentences; they had been acquitted. Rather, the report addressed the potential chilling effect and misuse
of criminal process. This follow-up retains that historical attribution and assigns no new grade. 1

SUBSEQUENT DEVELOPMENTS
The government's account describes the appellate court's acceptance of good-faith public criticism. This is
a government response hosted by UNESCO, not the judgment itself. Although its title names an April
2020 UN communication, its contents extend into 2021; its precise publication date is unstated. 2
The separate case documented in CFJ's 2024 report concerned 2014 posts criticizing Thairath reporter
Surapon Naknakorn's alleged conflict of interest. A complaint followed in September 2020 and an
indictment in September 2022. The 2023 settlement provided for withdrawal of civil and criminal claims in
exchange for an apology, publication of that apology, and 100,000 baht compensation. CFJ reports that
the defendants accepted because they no longer wished to continue going to court. Different speech,
complainant, and co-defendant establish that this was another proceeding. 4
