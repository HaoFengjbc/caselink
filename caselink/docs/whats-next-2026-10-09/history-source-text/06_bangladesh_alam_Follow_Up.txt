against Shahidul Alam, corroborated by contemporaneous local reporting and Amnesty the following day.

BACKGROUND AND PROCEDURAL HISTORY
Alam was arrested on 5 August 2018 after public commentary and an Al Jazeera interview concerning
student road-safety protests. A case under ICT Act Section 57 followed. The original examined the speech
allegations, detention, alleged torture and the consequences of Section 57's repeal and statutory savings
provisions. It argued that the prosecution should not continue where the relevant savings provision did not
preserve an investigation at that stage. That argument was not itself a court disposal. 1
The report also traced an earlier challenge: a 2021 High Court ruling on maintainability and rejection of
leave by the Appellate Division on 27 November 2022. Accordingly, when the original appeared in
January 2023, it could not report the later 2024 stay or 2025 quashing. References to the 2022 appeal in
subsequent coverage describe this earlier litigation, not a proven State appeal from the 2025 order. 1

SUBSEQUENT DEVELOPMENTS
Dhaka Tribune reported that the High Court suspended the investigation on 4 November 2024 and issued
a rule calling for justification of its continuation. A rule to show cause is not yet a final quashing order. The
report therefore supplies a genuine intermediate development without establishing that the prosecution
had already ended in 2024. 2
New Age reported that the High Court quashed the case on 7 August 2025. Bangla-language Ajker
Patrika independently reported the order, identified the bench of Justices Mustafa Zaman Islam and
Tamanna Rahman Khalidi, and described the rule as made absolute. These accounts support an actual
judicial disposal, not simply counsel's hope that the proceedings would be withdrawn. The signed reasons
were not obtained, so the precise ratio and operative scope should not be reconstructed from the original
report's legal argument. 3 5
Amnesty welcomed the dismissal on 8 August and reiterated its assessment that the prosecution arose
from protected expression. It also called for remedies for the arrest and alleged torture. Those are
attributed NGO assessments and demands, not a demonstrated judicial award. Amnesty's chronology
distinguishes the 15 November 2018 bail order from release on 20 November, an important separation
when describing liberty. 4

CONCLUSION AND OUTSTANDING RECORDS
The follow-up should report the verified 7 August 2025 quashing prominently, while qualifying any
statement about subsequent appellate finality. It should preserve the distinction between the earlier
release on bail, termination of the case and unresolved accountability claims. 3 4
RECORDS NOT VERIFIED
1. Signed 7 August 2025 High Court judgment, case number, reasons and exact operative scope.
2. Whether the State filed a leave-to-appeal application against that order, and whether any stay or
subsequent decision was issued.
3. Any compensation, torture investigation or other remedial proceedings and their outcomes.
4. Current registry confirmation rather than reliance on older appeals or retrospective NGO summaries.
Failure to locate an update leaves a gap in the available evidence; it does not establish that no later proceeding or change
occurred. Any present-status claim should be read with the dated scope stated in the executive summary.
