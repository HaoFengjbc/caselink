BACKGROUND AND PROCEDURAL HISTORY
The July 2019 report concerned access by TUT.BY staff to material from the state-owned BeLTA news
agency using shared subscription credentials. BeLTA temporarily restricted access to some stories before
making them generally available. Zolotova, TUT.BY's editor-in-chief, was prosecuted for inaction by an
official, based on an alleged failure to prevent the practice. Other people initially investigated were not
tried alongside her. After a two-week trial in February 2019, she was convicted and fined rather than
sentenced to imprisonment. 1
ABA Center for Human Rights staff, acting as TrialWatch experts, assigned a C. The report considered
the conduct of the trial largely fair but identified concerns about the purpose of singling out Zolotova and
the reliability of pretrial statements. It could not assess a published reasoned judgment at the time. This
historical report did not address the later raid on TUT.BY, the subsequent detention, or the closed
national-security prosecution. 1

SUBSEQUENT DEVELOPMENTS
Zolotova was arrested in May 2021, well after the monitored case. On 17 March 2023, CPJ reported a
twelve-year sentence for inciting hatred and distributing calls aimed at harming national security following
a closed trial. The initial tax-evasion allegation against her had been dropped. It is therefore inaccurate to
describe her twelve-year sentence as either punishment for the 2019 BeLTA charge or a tax-evasion
conviction. 2
CPJ's 15 December 2025 report confirmed that the release had occurred on 13 December, not merely
that it was expected. It reported presidential pardons for the released group and placed Zolotova among
them. CPJ welcomed her release while maintaining that her imprisonment was unjust. Its account supplies
evidence of actual liberty from prison; it does not supply a judgment reversing the conviction. 3
In the RSF Switzerland account, Zolotova described being free since December but unable to return to
her previous home. She explained the transfer through Ukraine into the European Union, obtaining
international protection in Poland and reuniting with her family. This first-person account materially
updates the immediate release announcement: it documents her circumstances months later rather than
assuming that a pardon restored residence, work and family life in Belarus. 4
Chekina's situation must be dated separately. Although she remained imprisoned when the August
account appeared, CPJ reported her release on 16 September among three freed journalists. The later
event supersedes earlier descriptions of that co-defendant's custody. It should not be recast as a
September release for Zolotova herself or as termination of the TUT.BY prosecution. 5

CONCLUSION AND OUTSTANDING RECORDS
The verified outcome is freedom from imprisonment followed by documented life in exile, not an acquittal.
The original C remains an attributed historical assessment of the 2019 proceedings; the later twelve-year
sentence and presidential pardon require their own clearly separated account.
RECORDS NOT VERIFIED
1. Published reasoned judgment and any appeal outcome in the 2019 BeLTA-related case.
2. Full judgment and verified completed appeal or review in the separate 2023 prosecution; a reported
intention to appeal is insufficient.
3. Individual pardon instrument and its effects on convictions, civil disabilities, travel documents and return
rights.
4. Any post-August 2026 substantive update on Zolotova's own legal status, distinct from Chekina's
September release.
Failure to locate an update leaves a gap in the available evidence; it does not establish that no later proceeding or change
occurred. Any present-status claim should be read with the dated scope stated in the executive summary.
