SUBSEQUENT DEVELOPMENTS
The 2022 operative order strikes Section 20 only insofar as it addresses reputational harm and the
corresponding punishment. The official consolidated text still prints that phrase alongside intimidation and
privacy, so printed consolidation alone does not establish that judicial invalidation was reversed. Its
natural-person wording also remains important: a general definition of 'person' cannot silently replace a
more specific element. 2 4
Act II of 2025 creates Section 26A for intentional dissemination of information known, or reasonably
believed, to be false or fake and likely to cause public fear, panic, disorder or unrest; the maximum is
three years or PKR 2 million, or both. It establishes the NCCIA statutory framework and social-media
regulatory machinery. The amended Section 43 places Sections 20 and 26A in the cognizable,
non-bailable and non-compoundable category. The Act does not delete Section 20 wholesale. Regulatory
content-blocking powers are distinct from that section's criminal elements. 3 4
The News reported in February 2026 that the IHC was hearing journalists' constitutional challenges and
had adjourned them to 6 March. The reported refusal to suspend legislation by interim stay is not a final
merits ruling upholding every provision. No later merits disposition of those petitions was verified in this
research. 5
A July update reports that the IHC ordered the NCCIA to disclose allegations before investigating Toor
under Section 26A. Separately, September reporting records Ghauri's bail on PKR 100,000 surety,
conditional on his not being required in another case. Bail does not terminate the prosecution; the precise
later custody position is not established here. These are new proceedings involving subjects named in the
original, not demonstrated dispositions of their original files. 6 7
