organizations, United Nations materials and attributable reporting available through 9 October 2026.

BACKGROUND AND PROCEDURAL HISTORY
This report concerns the prosecution initiated after Radi's arrest on 29 July 2020, involving
national-security and sexual-assault allegations. On 19 July 2021, the court convicted him of the charged
offenses, sentenced him to six years' imprisonment, and ordered 200,000 Moroccan dirhams in
compensation to the complainant. The original records an appellate judgment upholding the result on 3
March 2022. It is separate from the March 2020 conviction for criticizing a judge in a tweet. 1
Professor Hannah R. Garry assigned the original proceedings a D grade. TrialWatch identified prolonged
pretrial detention, impaired access to defense evidence, limits on testing prosecution testimony, and
impartiality concerns. It specifically discussed the refusal to hear a Dutch diplomat, treatment of defense
witness Imad Stitou as a co-accused, and alleged smear campaigns. These are the report's findings and
assessments, not a later acquittal or a judicial determination that all allegations were fabricated. 1

SUBSEQUENT DEVELOPMENTS
RSF reported that the Court of Cassation rejected Radi's and Soulaimane Raissouni's challenges on 18
July 2023, following earlier postponements. This supplies the principal post-report judicial outcome. RSF
criticized the proceedings and sought a royal pardon; those advocacy assessments must remain
attributed. A reported cassation rejection should not be expanded into an assertion that every possible
extraordinary or international remedy had been exhausted. 2
PEN confirmed release under the royal pardon granted on 29 July 2024. CPJ subsequently recorded
threatening calls to Radi. By March 2025, PEN's case profile still treated the effect on conviction as
unclear. The publicly verified change is therefore liberty through clemency, not an established reversal or
deletion of the conviction. 3 4 5
Amnesty's 1 October 2026 investigation reports a whistleblower's account of microphones in Radi's
apartment. It says key testimony was corroborated, notes earlier Moroccan denials, and records no
response to its inquiry. These are attributed NGO findings concerning historical surveillance, not a court
judgment or proof of renewed detention in 2026. 6
LEGAL AND PROCEDURAL ASSESSMENT
The following assessment draws inferences from the cited record. It does not represent a new court finding or TrialWatch
grade.
the sequence has two different legal meanings. The 2023 cassation rejection left the challenged judgment
undisturbed through that route. The 2024 pardon brought release. Without the individualized pardon
instrument or a later judgment, the research cannot establish whether the conviction, collateral
restrictions, or private compensation obligation were removed. Those questions must not be answered by
substituting the language of acquittal for clemency. 1 2 3 5
later surveillance material is relevant to the original report's concerns about the environment surrounding
the prosecution, but does not itself decide evidentiary issues in the sexual-assault case. Protecting that
distinction avoids treating an NGO investigation as a merits judgment. Similarly, reported intimidation after
release supports concern about practical freedom without proving imprisonment continued. 1 4 6

CONCLUSION AND OUTSTANDING RECORDS
The supported update is cassation rejection, followed by pardon and verified release, with later reports of
pressure and no verified vacatur. The original D grade remains an attributed historical assessment. A
claim of conviction clearing, compensation cancellation, or a final UN remedy would require
documentation not located in this research. 1 2 3 5
RECORDS NOT VERIFIED
1. Obtain the Court of Cassation judgment and the individualized royal pardon instrument, rather than
relying solely on reported outcomes.
2. Was the conviction subsequently quashed or expunged, and did professional or travel restrictions
remain? PEN's March 2025 profile did not resolve the legal effect.
3. What became of the 200,000-dirham compensation order? Release does not establish cancellation of
the civil obligation.
4. Did the UN Working Group on Arbitrary Detention issue an opinion specifically in Radi's case? No
authenticated opinion or disposition was located; do not substitute opinions concerning other Moroccan
journalists.
5. Were subsequent privacy, surveillance, or rehabilitation claims filed and adjudicated? The 2026
investigation is not evidence of such a court decision.
Failure to locate an update leaves a gap in the available evidence; it does not establish that no later proceeding or change
occurred. Any present-status claim should be read with the dated scope stated in the executive summary.
