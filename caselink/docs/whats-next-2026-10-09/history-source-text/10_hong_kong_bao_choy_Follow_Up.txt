BACKGROUND AND PROCEDURAL HISTORY
The July 2021 report concerned Choy's use of vehicle-registration records for an RTHK documentary
investigating the 21 July 2019 Yuen Long attacks and the police response. The application form required
a declared purpose; she selected the catch-all category for other traffic-and-transport-related matters.
Authorities subsequently treated that declaration as false. She was arrested in November 2020, convicted
on two false-statement charges in 2021 and fined HK$6,000. Her appeal was pending at the report's
publication. 1
The original expert assigned a C. TrialWatch considered courtroom procedures generally fair, while
identifying concerns about the legal clarity of the form and offence and the apparent selective targeting of
public-interest journalism. Those concerns were an assessment of the monitored trial, not a prediction of
the later appellate result. The follow-up therefore distinguishes the original grading exercise from the
binding interpretation subsequently supplied by the Court of Final Appeal. 1

SUBSEQUENT DEVELOPMENTS
The Court of First Instance initially dismissed the criminal appeal on 7 November 2022 in HCMA
236/2021, [2022] HKCFI 3343. It accepted a narrower relationship between the permitted search purpose
and road-traffic use. This intermediate result matters because the eventual appellate success was not the
first response to the conviction. 2
In FACC 2/2023, [2023] HKCFA 12, the court held that the catch-all purpose encompassed bona fide
journalism. Even on the narrower interpretation, the ambiguous form did not establish guilty knowledge as
the only reasonable inference. Materiality to the Commissioner's function did not itself prove falsity or
knowledge. The operative order quashed both convictions and set aside the sentence. 3
The Transport Department's January 2024 statement described a redesigned application regime with
specified purposes and written applications for exceptional public-interest uses. It presented the
arrangements as balancing privacy and access, and as consistent with the earlier judgment. That is the
government's stated rationale, not a determination that every later journalistic request must be approved.
4
The later judicial review, HCAL 559/2024, [2026] HKCFI 917, concerned HKJA and the Commissioner for
Transport, not new false-statement charges against Choy. On 6 March 2026 the court dismissed the
challenge. It identified errors in guidance and declarations requiring correction but did not grant the
review. The official Department of Justice summary separately records the policy's January 2024
implementation and the review's disposition. 5 6

CONCLUSION AND OUTSTANDING RECORDS
The original prosecution ended in the verified quashing of both convictions. Continuing disputes over
journalists' registry access are important follow-up context, but remain a separate legal controversy with a
separate judgment and unresolved questions about implementation.
RECORDS NOT VERIFIED
1. Whether HKJA appealed the March 2026 judicial-review judgment and any later disposition before 9
October.
2. Implementation and timing of the corrections to guidance and declarations identified by the 2026 court.
3. Any verified fine refund, compensation or other personal remedial order beyond the CFA's quashing
order.
4. Practical outcomes of Choy's or other journalists' applications under the revised policy; no
unrestricted-access conclusion is supported.
Failure to locate an update leaves a gap in the available evidence; it does not establish that no later proceeding or change
occurred. Any present-status claim should be read with the dated scope stated in the executive summary.
