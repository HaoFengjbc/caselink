EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
LATEST VERIFIED POSITION
December 2021 conviction appealed; appellate result unverified
Latest dated follow-up is an NGO statement announcing an expected 12 October 2022 ruling, not verification that
the ruling occurred. Sources disagree on the December conviction and appeal dates. Amnesty describes a
suspended custodial sentence; earlier sources describe provisional liberty. No current custody status is
established.
Unlike the June 2021 pre-disposition report, later sources establish a December 2021 conviction under
Article 86 of the Telecommunications Code, a four-month custodial sentence and a TND 500 fine,
followed by an appeal. Amnesty described the prison term as suspended and reported a prosecution
appeal as well. The latest dated follow-up located, published on 9 October 2022, announced an expected
ruling on 12 October; the ruling itself was not verified. 3 5 6
The proper conclusion is not acquittal, imprisonment served, or a final conviction. The subsequent
appellate result and enforcement position remain unresolved in the researched record through 9 October
2026. The supplied report assigned no letter grade. 1

BACKGROUND AND PROCEDURAL HISTORY
Bribri, a blogger and activist documenting police abuses, faced proceedings over a Facebook video and
accompanying criticism of police violence in October 2020. The original traced the complaint, questioning
and successive hearing adjournments, with another hearing scheduled for 28 June 2021. At publication, it
did not record a conviction. 1
The report examined Article 86's prohibition of knowingly harming others or disturbing their peace through
public telecommunications networks. Its concerns included the criminalization of political criticism, the
breadth of the offense and effective preparation of the defense. These historical observations should not
be silently converted into a judgment about the later appellate proceedings, whose records have not been
obtained. 1

SUBSEQUENT DEVELOPMENTS
HRW's January 2022 account, based in part on contact with Bribri, documented a conviction and
provisional liberty pending appeal. The February UN communication likewise recorded an appeal and
raised alleged restrictions on access to prosecution evidence. This was an actual communication by
special rapporteurs to Tunisia, not merely a proposed submission, but it was not a judicial finding that
Tunisia had violated a treaty. 2 3
Front Line Defenders' September notice confirms an announced appeal session. Amnesty's
contemporaneous statement adds two legally important qualifications: it describes the sentence as
suspended and says both defense and prosecution appealed. A suspended prison term is not
imprisonment already served, and a prosecution appeal makes it unsafe to presume that only a reduction
or acquittal was in issue. The exact procedural scope requires the appeal records. 4 5
The October WJWC statement supplies a later scheduled decision date, not the decision's contents.
Searches under Myriam, Meriem, Breibri and Arabic spellings did not establish a certified or reliably
reported appellate outcome. The absence of a located result is a research limitation, not evidence that the
case had no later developments. 6

enforcement has been verified. The follow-up should present that substantive development alongside an
