BACKGROUND AND PROCEDURAL HISTORY
The case concerned five reader comments posted under a June 2020 Malaysiakini article about the
reopening of courts. On 19 February 2021, the Federal Court found the publishing company guilty of
contempt by a six-to-one majority and imposed a RM 500,000 fine; it acquitted Gan. The original followed
the July 2020 merits hearing and examined the subsequent judgment. 1
The report criticized reliance on the Evidence Act Section 114A publication presumption and the treatment
of a platform's responsibility for third-party comments. It analyzed whether the procedure and penalty
complied with presumption-of-innocence and free-expression standards, including necessity and
proportionality. Its international-law analysis did not make Malaysia an ICCPR state party. These are
attributed TrialWatch criticisms, not findings adopted in the later review ruling. 1
The original stated that a review hearing was expected on 12 October 2021. That was an anticipated
procedural date, rather than a disposition. A follow-up must therefore identify the eventual review result
and not merely repeat the scheduled hearing as though it resolved the matter. 1

SUBSEQUENT DEVELOPMENTS
Malaysiakini's January report recorded counsel's confirmation of a new March hearing date. The eventual
March decision, rather than that announcement, provides the substantive procedural development after
the original report. This sequence explains why the October 2021 scheduled date cannot serve as the
endpoint of the follow-up. 4
Contemporaneous Bernama reporting states that the review panel found no breach of natural justice
warranting reopening and regarded the arguments as already considered. It also reports no costs order
because the matter involved public interest. Those conclusions explain rejection of this review request;
they should not be expanded into an independent finding that every criticism in the TrialWatch report was
answered. 2
The Vibes explains the restricted character of Federal Court review: it is not an ordinary appeal and does
not authorize another hearing simply because a party disagrees with the result. It reports that the
company complained about a lack of opportunity to address pivotal issues. The court nevertheless
rejected reopening. This is an unsuccessful procedural challenge, distinct from the original substantive
contempt adjudication. 3
