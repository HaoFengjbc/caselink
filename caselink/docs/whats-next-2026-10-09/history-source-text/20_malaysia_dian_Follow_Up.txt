BACKGROUND AND PROCEDURAL HISTORY
The original, dated 24 March 2021, examined proceedings over two Malaysia Flip Flop posts in March
2020 concerning the king's appointment of Muhyiddin Yassin and the prime minister's pandemic
response. Charges brought in June 2020 relied on Penal Code Section 505(b) and Communications and
Multimedia Act Section 233(1)(a). Bail was set at RM 4,000. 1
Trial hearings began in December 2020. The preliminary report questioned whether testimony established
public harm or incitement, rather than official discomfort with critical language. It recorded another hearing
scheduled for 25 March 2021; that schedule did not determine the eventual outcome. The international
free-expression standards discussed in the report should not be presented as proof that Malaysia is an
ICCPR state party. 1

SUBSEQUENT DEVELOPMENTS
The sentencing report separates the fines: RM 4,000 for the Section 233 count and RM 6,000 for Section
505(b), with three and six months' imprisonment respectively in default. It does not establish that the fine
was paid or default custody occurred. The verified change is therefore a reported plea-based conviction
and financial sentence, not an acquittal or confirmed period of imprisonment. 3
The 2025 Court of Appeal judgment in Quah found the pre-amendment criminalization of offensive
communications intended to annoy inconsistent with constitutional free-expression guarantees. Its
decision was prospective. That limitation alone prevents treating the ruling as an automatic reopening of
earlier convictions, and the constitutional position subsequently changed on appeal. 4
The Federal Court partly allowed the government's appeal in February 2026. It treated the abstract
constitutional challenge as inappropriate after Quah's discharge and the prosecution's no-further-action
decision, and upheld the impugned statutory language. Nevertheless, it found her particular post
protected and lacking the relevant intent. The case therefore does not authorize an inference that every
critical or disagreeable communication satisfies Section 233. 5

CONCLUSION AND OUTSTANDING RECORDS
The personal case progressed beyond the preliminary report to a reported guilty plea and sentence. Any
follow-up should record that disposition while leaving payment, enforcement and further challenge open.
Later constitutional litigation belongs in a separately identified legal-framework section.
