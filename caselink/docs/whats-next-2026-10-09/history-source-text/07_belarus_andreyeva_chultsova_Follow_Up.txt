LATEST VERIFIED POSITION
Chultsova released in 2022; Andreyeva pardoned and released in March
2026
Latest dated follow-up is PEN International's 24 March response to the 19 March release. Chultsova's
September 2022 release followed completion of her term; Andreyeva's 2026 release followed a pardon after a
separate treason conviction. Neither event establishes that either original conviction was quashed.
Katsiaryna Andreyeva was pardoned and released on 19 March 2026, after more than five years in
custody. CPJ confirmed her transfer to Lithuania; PEN International's 24 March statement subsequently
addressed the release and exile of the freed group. This was not a 2025 pardon or an acquittal. 5 6
Daria Chultsova had already been released in September 2022 after serving her sentence. A September
2025 Belarusian Association of Journalists report placed her in Poland. The defendants therefore have
different post-report trajectories, despite sharing the prosecution monitored in 2021. 4 7

BACKGROUND AND PROCEDURAL HISTORY
The October 2021 report examined the prosecution arising from the journalists' live coverage of a
memorial protest for Raman Bandarenka on 15 November 2020. Andreyeva was reporting and Chultsova
operating the camera from an apartment overlooking the gathering. The Frunzensky District Court in
Minsk convicted both under Article 342, concerning actions grossly violating public order, and imposed
two-year prison sentences on 18 February 2021. 1
TrialWatch expert Beth Van Schaack assigned the monitored proceedings a D. The report questioned the
equation of journalism with protest organization, the evidentiary basis for attributing transport disruption to
the defendants, restrictions on defense questioning and documentary evidence, and judicial impartiality
and the presumption of innocence. That assessment concerned the February 2021 hearings. It did not
monitor the later closed treason proceedings against Andreyeva. 1

SUBSEQUENT DEVELOPMENTS
Andreyeva's continued imprisonment was not simply the original two-year term running its course. CPJ
reported that a court in Homel convicted her of treason on 13 July 2022 after a closed trial. Details of the
alleged conduct were not publicly established in that account. The additional prosecution must be treated
independently of the public-order charges and the evidence examined by TrialWatch. 2
On 20 September 2022, BAJ reported an unsuccessful Supreme Court appeal and an effective sentence
of eight years and three months. Citing Andreyeva's husband, it said the description of the treason
allegation changed from disclosure of state secrets to espionage without changing the outcome. This is an
attributed account, not independent access to the sealed judgment. 3
BAJ confirmed Chultsova's release in early September 2022 after completion of her sentence. Public
sources vary over the precise day; this report uses the month rather than converting BAJ's publication
date into a release date. Her later presence in Poland is separately documented, rather than inferred from
the original sentence length. 4 7
CPJ linked the March release to a negotiated package and confirmed Andreyeva's pardon and transfer to
Lithuania without passports. PEN International welcomed release but characterized the accompanying
exile as coercive. These assessments are attributed to those organizations. 5 6
LEGAL AND PROCEDURAL ASSESSMENT
The following assessment draws inferences from the cited record. It does not represent a new court finding or TrialWatch
grade.

CONCLUSION AND OUTSTANDING RECORDS
Both defendants have verified releases, reached through different procedures. The follow-up should
preserve the original attributed grade, describe the later treason case separately, and leave the continuing
legal effects of both convictions expressly unresolved.
RECORDS NOT VERIFIED
1. Exact day of Chultsova's September 2022 release: contemporaneous accounts and later profiles differ
between 3 and 5 September.
2. Full judgments and publicly reviewable evidence in Andreyeva's treason proceedings, including the
precise calculation of the reported eight-year-three-month effective sentence.
3. Scope and legal conditions of Andreyeva's individual pardon, including whether criminal records, civil
restrictions or return rights changed.
4. Any subsequent judgment vacating either defendant's 2021 conviction; no such disposition was verified
through the cutoff.
Failure to locate an update leaves a gap in the available evidence; it does not establish that no later proceeding or change
occurred. Any present-status claim should be read with the dated scope stated in the executive summary.
